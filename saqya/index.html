Behind the message

canal turn (water in the canal) water in the root zone next days, following the advice if the farmer skips stress line

1. Weather, not guesswork

Every day Saqya pulls the free 16-day forecast for the farmer's village and computes how much water the crop will use (FAO-56 Penman-Monteith reference evapotranspiration times the crop coefficient for its growth stage).

2. A soil water account

It keeps a running balance of the water left in the root zone, like a bank account: rain and irrigation are deposits, crop water use is the withdrawal, and the red line is the minimum balance before the crop suffers.

3. Planned around the canal

In the old lands water only comes on the canal's turn. Before each turn Saqya asks one question: what is the least water that keeps this crop above the red line until the next turn? Then it texts the answer as pump time.

Saqya is a student project by Eyad Selim (Cairo) for the Xylem Global Student Innovation Challenge 2026, Water Quantity track. Weather data: Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0): forecast models for coming days, ERA5 reanalysis for past days. Method: FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 56 (Allen et al., 1998). In replay mode Saqya sees the real weather of the next days, so it shows the advice it would give with a perfect forecast. Source code.
